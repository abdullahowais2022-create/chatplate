# Motion-design techniques in top-tier SaaS / app product ads (2024–2026), for rebuilding a 45–60 s TillText ad in HTML/CSS/JS

Scope note: researched Nov 2026. Several useful hosts (designshack.net, open-design.ai) were blocked for direct fetch in this session; some were reached through Firecrawl's scraper instead. Brand/studio-specific breakdowns (Stripe, Linear, Notion, Revolut, Monzo, WhatsApp) were scarce in the sources found; the one strong 2026 breakdown is Apple's "Creator Studio" launch film, analysed by two independent sources. Items marked "practitioner default (unsourced)" in the Inferences sections are standard motion-design craft numbers that I could not tie to a citable source in this session. Treat them as starting values, not findings.

## 1. Who makes the most-praised product motion ads, and what techniques recur in them?

### Takeaway
The best-documented recent reference is Apple's 35-second "Introducing Apple Creator Studio" launch film (2026). It uses no voiceover, white-on-black kinetic type that physically *does* the verb it names, cuts locked to the music's percussion every 0.2–0.5 s, real UI elements, and a static price/logo hold at the end. Duolingo shows the character/brand-lore end of the range (TikTok-first serialized animation). In Aug 2026 Duolingo bought the studio Animade. BUCK and Giant Ant are the studios most often cited for high-end brand motion.

### Cited Findings
- Apple Creator Studio launch film: 35 s runtime, no voiceover. It is set in a "high-contrast digital void where typography and UI components behave as physical matter." — [AdsTurbo breakdown](https://adsturbo.ai/explore/954)
- Beat structure of that film: 0:00–0:05 the hook, where kinetic type morphs into "Creators Do" and snaps into the brand lockup. 0:05–0:14 multi-tool UI proof: a floating dock triggers windows, then a laptop-to-tablet morph. 0:14–0:22 rapid-fire action verbs. 0:22–0:28 app icons converge, showing that the tools are bundled. 0:28–0:35 the price anchor plus logo, "holding static for clear retention." — [AdsTurbo breakdown](https://adsturbo.ai/explore/954)
- "Cut lengths are restricted to 0.2 to 0.5 seconds, locking visual state changes directly to the electronic music track's percussion." Composition is "rigid center-weighted" and transitions use "rapid axial zoom-outs, split wipes, and precise jump cuts." — [AdsTurbo breakdown](https://adsturbo.ai/explore/954)
- "Embodied" type, where the text physically performs its own verb: "'Prompt it' disintegrates into particulate dust, 'Scrap it' crumples like paper into a frosted trash bin, and 'Upscale it' is cut by a real-time resolution slider." — [AdsTurbo breakdown](https://adsturbo.ai/explore/954)
- The laptop rotates and compresses into a tablet "in under one second." The staging is pure black, with rim light on hardware edges and self-illuminated neon UI in cyan, ultraviolet and electric green. — [AdsTurbo breakdown](https://adsturbo.ai/explore/954)
- Copy is built from active verbs (Perform, Write, Upscale, Share) rather than feature lists. — [AdsTurbo breakdown](https://adsturbo.ai/explore/954)
- From an independent analysis of the same film by Good Boy Ninja:
  - "Make a beat" is on screen for about 16 frames, the shortest title in the film.
  - It works because the Logic Pro icon "jumps" just before, pulling the eye to centre screen where the text appears.
  - The text breaks apart left-to-right, so the last word is still read.
  - The phrase is only three words long.
- Same analysis: in another moment Apple holds "Prompt it" longer than needed to read it, then uses match cuts to restyled versions of the same phrase. The viewer re-reads nothing and only takes in the visuals. The lesson drawn is that title length should depend on "how long does it take us to communicate it," not only on reading time. — [Good Boy Ninja](https://www.goodboy.ninja/blog/post/lets-analyze-apples-creator-studio-motion-graphics-video)
- Same analysis: "Apple always starts with white on black titles". Colour and motion are then added only to emphasise each title's message. "Almost never does Apple use colors and motion as a way to impress you." — [Good Boy Ninja](https://www.goodboy.ninja/blog/post/lets-analyze-apples-creator-studio-motion-graphics-video)
- Same analysis, on the opening screenshot sweep: screenshots speed toward screen-right, and just before exiting they move slightly *left* (anticipation). The individual panes have deliberately inconsistent easing; some move uniformly, some get an "extra boost" at the end, some feel "jumpy". This gives a near-handheld, analogue feel. — [Good Boy Ninja](https://www.goodboy.ninja/blog/post/lets-analyze-apples-creator-studio-motion-graphics-video)
- Apple's recap videos use Rapid Serial Visual Presentation (RSVP): words flashed one after another in the same spot, cutting eye movement so the video can be understood with the sound off. The example cited is "Apple – Don't Blink". The source is a 2023 agency blog, so this is an older classic. — [Optious](https://www.optious.com/apples-secret-weapon-kinetic-typography/)
- Duolingo (TikTok Ad Awards case study, "Duolingo Cult Mass", Germany):
  - Serialized TikTok-first brand entertainment built around Duo's "death" and revival as a cult leader.
  - Mixed immersive POV content with cinematic scenes, and used Spark Ads to scale the strongest posts.
  - Results: 34.83M impressions, 26.89M reach, 12.63% engagement rate.
  - [TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/inspiration/ad-awards-duolingo-case-study)
- Duolingo's 2024 animated ad was praised for using the same characters and visuals as the app, and everyday scenarios like ordering a croissant or getting a haircut. — [Squideo](https://www.squideo.com/the-top-5-animated-ads-of-2024-and-why-they-worked)
- School of Motion's Motion Mondays (Aug 24 2026) reported "Duolingo Buys Animade." Show notes link to Animade's "over-and-owlt" post. — [School of Motion / YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qkh6Cv04y1U)
- A 2026 list of motion studios cites Giant Ant, with named clients OpenTable, Duolingo and Microsoft, and BUCK ("ambitious, high-end motion and animation for major brand campaigns"). — [Moonb](https://www.moonb.io/blog/motion-graphics-companies)
- Ordinary Folk (Vancouver) made conference-opener videos for Elastic. I found no source linking it to Stripe or Airbnb. — [Inspiration Grid](https://theinspirationgrid.com/elastic-conference-animated-videos-by-ordinary-folk)
- Airbnb's motion legacy includes Lottie, its After Effects → code export. That matters here because Airbnb's product motion was designed to run as code. — [School of Motion](https://schoolofmotion.com/blog/after-effects-to-code-lottie-from-airbnb)
- Freelance SaaS launch-video market descriptions emphasise:
  - "UI-focused motion built around the actual product interface, with tight pacing and intentional transitions".
  - An "Abstracted UI" style as a leading SaaS trend.
  - These are low-authority sources (Dribbble/LinkedIn listings). — [Dribbble](https://dribbble.com/services/230343-Saas-Product-Promo-Video), [Contra](https://contra.com/p/lRvPgNvl-product-launch-videos-motion-design-for-ai-and-saa-s-companies)

### Inferences
- The Apple formula maps directly onto TillText's four claims. Each becomes an "embodied verb" beat where the word performs the feature:
  - "Answers": the word types itself into a WhatsApp bubble with a typing indicator.
  - "Takes orders": the word becomes a line item with a price that ticks in.
  - "Shows them all": order cards fly into a dashboard grid.
  - "Understands any language": the word cycles through scripts, e.g. "Orders → Pedidos → Bestellungen → 订单 → طلبات".
  - "Voice notes": a waveform turns into transcribed text.
- "Abstracted UI" is likely the safest route for an HTML build. That means simplified, chunky versions of WhatsApp bubbles and dashboard cards, not pixel-perfect screenshots. It reads at small sizes and avoids implying a WhatsApp/Meta endorsement. This is an inference; also check Meta's brand-use rules for the WhatsApp name and glyph.
- Build in Apple-style "tiny imperfections": vary the easing or delay of each element by a few frames in staggered groups. This avoids the robotic look of identical CSS transitions.

### Gaps
- I found no first-party or studio breakdowns of 2024–2026 ads by Stripe, Linear, Notion, Revolut, Monzo, Shopify, Mailchimp or WhatsApp/Meta. Searches returned only templates and freelancer pages imitating "Linear-style". Specific claims about those brands' techniques are therefore unsourced. Examples include Linear's dark gradient glow and grain, Stripe's animated gradient mesh, and Notion's hand-drawn black-line illustrations. They are commonly cited in design circles but unverified here.
- I could not confirm a Hyperframe-style AI motion studio from any source.
- ManvsMachine, Oddfellows and Animade had no product-ad case studies with technique detail in the results, beyond the Animade acquisition news.

## 2. Concrete techniques: kinetic type, UI screen choreography, transitions, easing, counters, bento grids

### Takeaway
The sourced, implementable core is:
- Kinetic type that transforms in place: RSVP, morphing letterforms, break-apart left-to-right, text that performs its verb.
- Center-weighted UI choreography: a dock triggers windows; axial zoom-outs from a detail to the whole workspace; split-slider wipes; device morphs.
- Match cuts on the same phrase.
- Anticipation before exits.
- Strong ease-out curves rather than CSS built-ins.

### Cited Findings
- Kinetic-type patterns in the Apple film:
  - Letterform morphing into a lockup within the first 3 s.
  - "Instant expansion from single letters to a multi-window pro workspace grid."
  - Text crumbling into particles or crumpling like paper.
  - Pixelated-to-vector text across a split-slider wipe.
  - "3D typographic elements bounce and slide across the grid".
  - App badges "slide inward on rhythmic beats to form a centralized cluster."
  - [AdsTurbo](https://adsturbo.ai/explore/954)
- Camera language in the same film: "rapid axial zoom-outs" (pull straight back from a detail to the whole layout), with close-ups alternating "instantly" with wider workspace grids. — [AdsTurbo](https://adsturbo.ai/explore/954)
- The opening sweep uses anticipation: panels move slightly opposite to their exit direction before exiting. — [Good Boy Ninja](https://www.goodboy.ninja/blog/post/lets-analyze-apples-creator-studio-motion-graphics-video)
- Match cuts on repeated text (same words, new styling) extend a hold without making viewers re-read. — [Good Boy Ninja](https://www.goodboy.ninja/blog/post/lets-analyze-apples-creator-studio-motion-graphics-video)
- Product-demo zoom practice:
  - Zoom factors of 150–300%.

  - "Most of the time an ease-out curve […] will be the best choice because the animation will kick off immediately."
  - "ease-in-out can be an appropriate choice when you're moving something that is already on screen."
- Overshoot ("back") easing: y control values above 1 or below 0 create overshoot or anticipation. A common overshoot curve is cubic-bezier(0.175, 0.885, 0.32, 1.275). A single cubic-bezier can overshoot only once; multi-bounce or elastic motion needs CSS `linear()` or a JS function. — [web.dev Custom easing](https://web.dev/articles/custom-easing); value also listed in search results for [Anime.js cubic-bezier docs](https://animejs.com/documentation/easings/cubic-bezier-easing/)

  - Durations: small elements 100 ms; medium 250 ms expanding / 200 ms collapsing; large 300 ms expanding / 250 ms collapsing.
  - These are UI-interaction speeds; ad motion is usually somewhat slower and more exaggerated.
  - [Material Design M2 Speed](https://m2.material.io/design/motion/speed.html)
- Trend-list techniques (2024 roundup; few examples given):

  - [Linearity, Mar 2024](https://www.linearity.io/blog/motion-design-trends/)
- Data-driven motion ("turning complex datasets into dynamic visual narratives") was predicted as a 2025 frontier. — [Motionographer 2025 Pt 1](https://motionographer.com/2024/12/10/motionographers-crystal-ball-2025-motion-design-predictions-part-1/)

### Inferences
Practitioner defaults (unsourced), translated to 30 fps frames unless noted. All timings come from one global frame clock: `t = frame / fps`, with a per-element `progress = ease(clamp((t - start) / dur))`. Never use CSS transition timers, because frame-by-frame rendering must be deterministic.

**Easing palette (3 curves is enough)**

| Use | Curve |
|---|---|
| Entrances | ease-out-expo (.19,1,.22,1) or out-quint (.23,1,.32,1) |
| Moving on-screen things / camera | in-out-quart (.77,0,.175,1) or in-out-cubic |
| Exits | ease-in-cubic over a shorter duration than the entrance (about 60–70%) |
| Pop or "punch" | back-out with c1 about 1.4–1.7 (10–15% overshoot) |

Back-out formula: `1 + (c1+1)(x-1)^3 + c1(x-1)^2`, with c1 = 1.70158 for the classic ~10% overshoot. Alternatively use a damped spring: `1 - e^(-ζωt)·cos(ωd·t)`, with ζ ≈ 0.5–0.7 and a settle time of about 400–600 ms.

**Durations at 30 fps**
- Small UI element pop: 8–12 frames (≈270–400 ms).
- Card or bubble slide-in: 12–18 f.
- Scene-wide camera push: 24–45 f.
- Full-screen transition wipe: 10–16 f.

**Stagger**
- Letters: 1–2 f per character.
- Words: 3–5 f.
- List items or cards: 3–6 f (≈100–200 ms).
- Total stagger for a group should stay under about 20 f, or it reads as slow.

**Kinetic-type recipes**
- *Mask reveal:* each line sits inside an `overflow:hidden` wrapper. It slides from translateY(110%) to 0 with out-expo over 14 f, staggered 4 f per line.
- *Scale punch:* the key word goes from scale 0.6→1.08→1.0 in 10 f (back-out), with an optional 2-frame background colour flash on the beat.
- *Variable-weight morph:* animate `font-variation-settings: 'wght'` from 300 to 800 over 12 f with a variable font such as Inter, Manrope or Roboto Flex. Pair it with letter-spacing going from 0.02em to -0.02em.
- *RSVP word swap:* one word replaces another in the same spot every 6–10 f, aligned to beats.
- *Language cycle:* swap the word on a vertical "slot machine" roll. Each word moves translateY(-100%) over 8 f, with a 2–3 f motion-blur smear (see below).

**UI choreography recipes**
- *Phone tilt:* the wrapper has `perspective:1600px`, and the phone uses `rotateY(-18deg) rotateX(8deg)`. It eases to 0 over 30 f as the camera pushes in from scale 0.85 to 1.0.
- *Parallax depth:* use 3 layers moving at 0.6× / 1× / 1.4× of the camera translate.
- *Chat bubble entry:*
  - Typing-dots bubble for 18–24 f; each dot bounces in a 12 f cycle, offset 4 f.
  - Then the message bubble scales from 0.8 at its tail-corner origin to 1.0 with back-out over 10 f, with opacity 0→1 over the first 4 f.
  - Neighbouring bubbles push up via translateY with in-out-cubic over 10 f.
- *Cursor/tap:*
  - The cursor travels on an in-out-cubic path over 15–25 f and decelerates onto the target.
  - Tap: the target scales to 0.94 over 3 f, then back to 1 over 6 f.
  - A ripple ring grows from scale 0→1.6 while opacity goes 0.5→0 over 12 f.
- *Counter:* tween a number with out-expo over 30–45 f. Format each frame with `Intl.NumberFormat`. Use tabular numerals (`font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`) so the width doesn't jitter.
- *Chart:* bars grow by scaleY from origin bottom, staggered 3 f, with out-quint over 18 f. Line charts draw via `stroke-dashoffset`.
- *Bento grid assemble:* cards arrive from slight offsets (±40 px, scale 0.92, opacity 0) with out-expo over 16 f. Stagger them in reading order or radially from the centre, 3–4 f apart. Finish with a 30 f slow camera drift of about 2–3% scale.
- *Axial zoom-out:* scale the whole layout from about 3.0 (one detail fills frame) to 1.0 over 24–36 f with in-out-quart. This is Apple's "detail → workspace" move.

**Transition recipes**
- *Match cut:* the last frame's key shape (e.g. a green bubble) and the next scene's key shape (e.g. a dashboard order card) share position, size and colour, then cut hard on a beat.
- *Shape morph:* tween `border-radius` and size from a circle to a rounded rect, or use SVG path interpolation such as flubber.js.
- *Blob/liquid wipe:* an SVG mask with an animated path, or a circle `clip-path` expanding (`circle(0% at x y)` → `circle(150%)`) over 12–16 f with in-out-cubic.
- *Split-slider wipe:* a `clip-path: inset()` reveal with a visible divider handle, Apple-style.

**Motion blur in HTML**
- Option 1: render N sub-frames (e.g. 8) per output frame at shutter 180°, i.e. spread across half the frame interval, and average them. This is accurate but costs N× render time.
- Option 2 (cheaper): fake it by applying a directional blur proportional to velocity only on fast moves, e.g. an SVG `feGaussianBlur stdDeviation="x,0"` plus a slight scaleX stretch.

### Gaps
- No source gave measured overshoot percentages, stagger offsets or durations from a named studio's ad. The numbers above are craft defaults, not findings.
- I could not retrieve Ben Marriott's or Jake in Motion's specific easing or graph-editor values in this session.

## 3. Timing rules: shot length, cuts per second, text hold times, hook

### Takeaway
- Platform guidance: state the proposition within 3 s and keep text overlays to 5–10 words per second (TikTok).
- Subtitle science offers a floor: Netflix caps adult reading speed at 20 characters per second.
- Apple's high-energy montage cuts every 0.2–0.5 s, but only for 1–3-word titles, after a slower intro, and with eye-guiding cues.
- A 45–60 s ad with voiceover needs slower holds than a music-only montage.

### Cited Findings
- TikTok official guidance:
  - "Introduce your content proposition in the first 3 seconds for better recall and awareness"; "Prioritize your hook in the first 6 seconds."
  - Vertical 9:16, at least 720p.
  - "We recommend displaying 5-10 words per second when using text."
  - Use transitions, stickers and graphics "to retain engagement"; end with a clear CTA.
  - [TikTok Ads Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices)
- Hook guidance from secondary sources: open on a problem, a bold claim or an unexpected visual, "never a logo," aiming for a distinctive beat in about 1–1.5 s. — [AdRoast](https://www.adroast.ai/blog/tiktok-ad-hooks-first-3-seconds). Hook formulas: "bold claim opens, mid-action opens, and direct address opens." — [Stackmatix 2026](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/tiktok-creative-best-practices-2026). Both are secondary or marketing sources.
- Netflix English (USA) timed text: up to 20 characters per second for adult programs, 17 for children's, 42 characters per line. — [Netflix Partner Help](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/217350977-English-USA-Timed-Text-Style-Guide)
- A 2026 guide on on-screen text duration sets durations from the "stable reading window" and cites Netflix's rules, including a minimum duration of "five-sixths of a second" (20 frames at 24 fps). — [OpenDesign, Nov 2026](https://open-design.ai/blog/on-screen-text-duration/)
- Subtitling's "6-second rule": a two-line subtitle of about 64 characters is shown for about 6 s (≈10.5 cps), the classic slower benchmark. Speech runs at about 120–240 wpm (2–4 words/s). — summarised from search results on [Szarkowska et al. / PLOS One](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6007935)
- Editorial heuristics, not research:
  - 1–2 word emphasis: 1–1.5 s.
  - 3–5 word callout: 1.5–2.5 s.
  - 6–8 word statement: 2.5–4 s.
  - A claimed BBC floor of about 0.3 s per word, which I could not verify.
  - From search results citing [Overseer OS](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/how-long-should-on-screen-text-stay-youtube)
- Apple Creator Studio:
  - 35 s total. Cuts of 0.2–0.5 s in the montage — [AdsTurbo](https://adsturbo.ai/explore/954).
  - The shortest title is about 16 frames, a 3-word phrase.
  - The intro is "the slowest part of the video," setting the tempo before the fast section.
  - [Good Boy Ninja](https://www.goodboy.ninja/blog/post/lets-analyze-apples-creator-studio-motion-graphics-video)
- Designers misjudge their own pacing: "What you think isn't too fast is probably too fast," because they've memorised the copy. — [Good Boy Ninja](https://www.goodboy.ninja/blog/post/lets-analyze-apples-creator-studio-motion-graphics-video)
- The close should settle on a static price/logo hold "for clear retention." — [AdsTurbo](https://adsturbo.ai/explore/954)

### Inferences
- **Hold-time formula for TillText** (derived from the cited numbers):
  - hold_seconds = max(0.85, chars / 15) + 0.3 s for the entrance to settle. 15 cps is a margin under Netflix's 20, for non-native readers and busy backgrounds.
  - Example: "Answers customers on WhatsApp" is 29 chars, so 1.9 s + 0.3 = about 2.2 s.
  - With VO, the text should be on screen at least as long as the spoken phrase.
- **Scene budget for a 50 s cut (16:9 or 9:16)**, about 9–12 scenes averaging 4–5 s, with internal micro-cuts or motion changes every 0.5–1.5 s so nothing is static for more than about 1.5 s:

  | Time | Beat |
  |---|---|
  | 0–3 s | Hook: the problem — phone buzzing with unanswered WhatsApp messages piling up, counter "47 unread" |
  | 3–6 s | Brand reveal: logo morphs out of a chat bubble |
  | 6–16 s | Feature 1: AI answers instantly (chat choreography) |
  | 16–24 s | Feature 2: takes orders with real prices (line items, total counter) |
  | 24–31 s | Feature 3: any language and voice notes (word cycle, waveform → text) |
  | 31–40 s | Feature 4: one dashboard (bento/card assemble, axial zoom-out) |
  | 40–47 s | Proof or outcome (counter or chart) |
  | 47–55/60 s | CTA with a static hold of ≥2.5 s and a URL |

  The energy curve is slower intro → faster middle montage → calm, static end card.
- Cut on musical beats. At 120 BPM a beat is 15 frames at 30 fps; at 100 BPM, 18 f. Schedule entrances so the end of the ease (the "settle") lands on the beat, not the start.
- Montage cuts of 0.2–0.5 s should be used only for 1–3-word titles or purely visual beats, and only once the viewer has the tempo.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable published statistic for "cuts per second" in SaaS explainer ads in general. Apple's 0.2–0.5 s is one high-energy example, not a norm.
- I could not fetch Wyzowl, Yum Yum Videos or Breadnbeyond explainer-length guidance in this session, so explainer-specific pacing norms (e.g. VO words per minute for explainers) remain unsourced. The commonly quoted figure of about 150 wpm is a practitioner default.

## 4. Trends 2025–2026 in product motion, and what is considered dated

### Takeaway
Sourced trends point to:
- Restraint: bold minimal type, negative space, restricted palettes.
- Real or abstracted product UI as the hero.
- Hybrid 2D/3D and tactile finishes: hand-drawn thin lines, liquid and biomorphic motion.
- Loopable micro-animations.
- Vertical-first composition.
- AI tools used in production rather than as a look.

Apple's 2026 film shows the premium end: pure black void, white type, colour only for emphasis, neon self-lit UI.

### Cited Findings
- Motionographer 2025 predictions, Pt 1:
  - "Minimalistic Aesthetic: bold typography, thoughtful negative space, and carefully curated color palettes as a response to digital clutter."
  - Hyperrealistic 3D via real-time engines (Unreal Engine 5), "blending photorealistic elements with surreal, playful concepts".
  - Data-driven design.
  - AI "amplifying human creativity rather than replacing it."
  - [Motionographer Pt 1](https://motionographer.com/2024/12/10/motionographers-crystal-ball-2025-motion-design-predictions-part-1/)
- Motionographer Pt 3:
  - Loopable animations "taking over social media, digital billboards, and app interfaces".
  - Biomorphic, organic motion.
  - Experimental virtual-camera techniques that "defy physics," with "unbroken motion journeys".
  - Micro-animations from UI/UX.
  - [Motionographer Pt 3](https://motionographer.com/2025/01/14/25-1-for-2025-the-motionographers-crystal-ball-motion-design-predictions-part-3)

  - Isometric flat-vector "corporate Memphis" people.
  - Stock template text presets such as typewriter-on-everything and bounce-in for every word.
  - Lens flares.
  - Opening on a logo, which the TikTok guidance warns against.
  - A 16:9 cut letterboxed into 9:16.
  - Isometric shapes appear on the 2024 trend list, so isometric is not uniformly dated. Flat corporate-Memphis characters are what is widely considered tired; that last point is unsourced.
- **Fitting 2025–26 look for TillText:**
  - Bold grotesk/sans display type, e.g. Inter Display, Manrope, General Sans or Satoshi, at weights 700–800 with tight tracking.
  - Brand-colour blocking: a WhatsApp-adjacent green used sparingly as the emphasis colour on a near-black or off-white field.
  - Abstracted UI cards with soft shadows and large radii (24–32 px), arranged in a bento layout for the dashboard scene.
  - Subtle film grain via an SVG `feTurbulence` noise overlay at 3–6% opacity. Animate its `seed` every 2 frames, or the grain looks frozen.
  - One tactile accent: hand-drawn thin-line circles or arrows drawn on with `stroke-dashoffset` around key numbers.
  - Glassmorphism via backdrop-filter is common in 2024–26 UI. In frame-rendered HTML, `backdrop-filter` can be slow or inconsistent across headless renderers, so test it.

## 5. Vertical 9:16 reel specifics vs 16:9

### Takeaway
On a 1080×1920 canvas, keep critical text and UI inside a central area of roughly 900×1400 px:
- About 210–270 px clear at the top.
- About 310–370 px clear at the bottom, since captions and the CTA bar cover it.
- About 120 px clear on the right, where the like/comment/share rail sits.

Compose center-weighted. TikTok recommends 9:16, ≥720p, the proposition within 3 s, and 5–10 words per second of text.

### Cited Findings
- Per-platform safe zones at 1080×1920 — [AdaptlyPost 2026 guide](https://adaptlypost.com/blog/social-media-safe-zones-2026-complete-guide):

  | Platform | Safe area | Top | Bottom | Left | Right |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | TikTok | 900×1492 | 108 | 320 (370 for ads) | 60 | 120 |
  | Instagram Reels | 996×1400 | 210 | 310 | — | 84 blocked |
  | YouTube Shorts | 984×1500 | 120 | 300 (360 with expanded description) | — | 96 |
  | Facebook Reels | 1080×1520 | 100 | 300 | — | — |

  The same guide suggests a shared conservative zone of about 900×1400 for cross-posting, and warns that enlarged captions or accessibility settings can take 250–350 px at the bottom.
- Conflicting Reels figures: Revid gives 895×980 px starting 270 px from the top and 65 px from the left, with cover text between 285 and 1250 px from the top. — [Revid](https://www.revid.ai/instagram-reels-safe-zone). Canadian Tire's brand system uses 48 px side margins and an 896×1264 type-safe area starting 260 px from the top. — [Canadian Tire brand design system](https://brand-qa2.canadiantire.ca/en/ctr/design-system/omni-channel/motion/margins-and-safe-space.html). Sources disagree, so use the most conservative overlap.
- TikTok specs: 9:16, ≥720p, content within the UI safe zone, 5–10 words/s for text, hook in the first 3–6 s, end with a CTA. — [TikTok Ads Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices)
- Apple's launch film is built on "rigid center-weighted composition." — [AdsTurbo](https://adsturbo.ai/explore/954)
- Vertical-first animation is a named trend: it is not just a crop of horizontal work. — [Linearity](https://www.linearity.io/blog/motion-design-trends/)
- Tools such as Adobe Express and Kapwing provide toggleable safe-zone overlays. — [Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/feature/design/safe-zone/social), [Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/video-editor/instagram-reels/safe-zone)

### Inferences
- **Text size at 1080 px wide** (practitioner default, unsourced):
  - Headlines 80–120 px (bold).
  - Secondary lines 48–64 px.
  - Nothing below about 36 px.
  - A useful check is that a headline should be legible on a phone held at arm's length when the frame is scaled to about 360 px wide, i.e. ≥ about 26 px effective.
  - In 16:9 at 1920×1080, headlines of 96–140 px work. The vertical frame needs similar absolute pixel sizes because it is viewed at phone size, not TV size.
- **Lines and words**
  - Vertical: at most 2–3 lines and about 14–18 characters per line for headlines.
  - 16:9: 1 line of up to about 30 characters.
- **Layout**
  - Vertical: stack UI vertically, with the phone mockup at about 70–80% of frame width centred between y≈260 and y≈1500. Put headline text above the phone, never in the bottom 350 px.
  - 16:9: use left text / right UI splits, the classic SaaS layout.
- **Build both from one scene graph:** define positions in safe-area-relative units, and swap layout presets per aspect ratio (e.g. `layout = aspect < 1 ? 'stack' : 'split'`). Keep identical timing so one audio/VO track drives both cuts.
- Keep the CTA and URL between y≈1100 and y≈1450 in 9:16. That sits above the TikTok ad CTA bar at about 370 px bottom and the Reels caption.
